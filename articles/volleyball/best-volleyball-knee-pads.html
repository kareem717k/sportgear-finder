Best Volleyball Knee Pads (2026)

Home / Volleyball / Best Volleyball Knee Pads (2026)

Volleyball knee pads exist for one reason: the floor. Hardwood sport courts are unforgiving on bare knees during digs, dives, and slides. The right knee pad protects without restricting movement — a pad that's too bulky slows you down, and one that's too thin barely helps. Here's what to buy at each level.

What to Look For in a Volleyball Knee Pad

  • Padding thickness: Liberos and defensive specialists who dive frequently need more padding (18–25mm foam). Setters and outside hitters who go down less often can use thinner low-profile pads without sacrificing much.
  • Sleeve length: Longer sleeves stay in place better through practice. Short sleeves slide down during intense play, which is more annoying than it sounds when you're mid-point.
  • Slide surface: Some pads have a slick external layer designed to reduce floor friction on digs. Worth prioritizing if you play on rough sport court surfaces.
  • Compression fit: The sleeve should feel firm but not restrictive. If blood flow feels reduced, size up.

Sizing Your Knee Pads

Measure the circumference of your thigh about 4 inches above the centre of your kneecap. This is the measurement most volleyball knee pad brands size against — not calf or calf-to-thigh length. When in doubt between two sizes, go larger: a slightly loose pad is easier to manage than one that restricts circulation during extended play.

FAQs

Do beginners need volleyball knee pads?

Yes — especially if you're playing on hardwood or sport court. Even basic technique involves dropping to your knees to pick up low balls, and court floors are hard. The injury risk is real and the pads are cheap relative to the protection they provide. Treat them as non-negotiable gear, not optional extras.

How long do knee pads last?

With regular use (3–4 sessions per week), foam padding compresses over 12–18 months and loses its protective quality. The sleeve elastic also degrades and the pad starts sliding. When either happens, replace them — compressed pads feel protective but aren't. The cost is low enough that annual replacement is reasonable for active players.

Can I use basketball or general sports knee pads?

Basketball pads tend to be larger and bulkier, which restricts the lateral movement volleyball requires. They'll protect you but will slow you down. Volleyball-specific pads are slimmer, longer-sleeved, and designed for the floor contact patterns specific to the sport. The price difference is minimal — buy volleyball pads.
