Best Volleyball Ankle Braces (2026)

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read · Affiliate links may earn us a commission

Home / Volleyball / Best Volleyball Ankle Braces (2026)

Ankle sprains are the most common volleyball injury, and they happen predictably: landing from a block or attack and coming down on an opponent's foot. An ankle brace won't make you jump higher, but it can be the difference between a rolled ankle that ends your season and one that costs you a single point. Here's what to buy at each level.

Lace-Up vs Rigid Hinged: Which Type Do You Need?

  • Lace-up braces (McDavid 195, ASO): Flexible, low-profile, fits inside any volleyball shoe without sizing up. Best for prevention — players with no significant ankle history who want insurance against the occasional bad landing. The most common type in recreational and club volleyball.
  • Rigid hinged braces (Zamst A2-DX, Ultra Zoom): Plastic stirrups restrict inversion/eversion more aggressively. Used for players returning from a significant sprain or with chronic instability. Bulkier — may require a half-size larger volleyball shoe.

For most volleyball players, a lace-up brace is the right choice. If you've had a Grade 2 or Grade 3 sprain, consult a physio and consider a hinged brace for the return-to-play period.

Best Overall — Prevention

McDavid 195 Ankle Brace with Figure-8 Straps

The most widely used volleyball ankle brace at club and collegiate level. The lace-up construction creates a firm, customizable fit that doesn't rely on one-size Velcro tabs, and the figure-8 strapping reinforces the ligaments most likely to roll. Low-profile enough to fit inside an ASICS Upcourt or Mizuno Wave Lightning without sizing up. Machine washable, which matters when you're wearing it four times a week. The McDavid 195 sits at $20–25 and is the recommendation for any player who wants reliable prevention without overbuilding the brace.

Best for Chronic Instability

Zamst A2-DX Ankle Brace

The Zamst A2-DX is what athletic trainers reach for when a player has significant ankle history. The Exo-Grid external support framework and dual-direction straps restrict both inversion and eversion — useful for players whose ankles roll in either direction, not just outward. The rigid components require sizing up half a shoe size to fit properly inside a volleyball shoe, and the application takes slightly longer than a lace-up, but the support level is meaningfully higher. At $65–75, it's a significant investment, but it protects against re-injury in a way a lace-up simply cannot.

ASO Ankle Stabilizing Orthosis

The ASO is a medical-grade lace-up brace commonly seen in athletic training rooms. Do You Need to Size Up Your Volleyball Shoe?

For lace-up braces (McDavid 195, ASO): usually no. These are thin enough to fit inside most volleyball shoes without noticeably affecting fit — though your shoe will feel slightly snugger, especially around the heel. If your shoes fit exactly true to size with no extra room, try the brace on in-store before committing.

For rigid hinged braces (Zamst A2-DX): yes, typically a half-size larger. The plastic components add meaningful volume to the heel and ankle area. Factor this into your shoe purchase if you plan to brace from day one.

FAQs

Should I wear an ankle brace on both ankles?

If you have history on one side, definitely brace that ankle. Bracing both is also reasonable — studies show that ankle braces reduce sprain rates on both the braced ankle and, to a lesser extent, the unbraced one (because they change how you land). Many competitive players brace both as a matter of course. The cost and minor inconvenience are small relative to the protection.

Will an ankle brace make my ankle weaker over time?

The evidence suggests no — wearing a brace during sport doesn't atrophy the supporting muscles if you maintain normal strength training. The old concern about braces causing dependency was based on earlier, more restrictive designs. Modern lace-up braces allow enough natural ankle movement that muscle function is maintained. Continue calf strengthening and balance work regardless.

Can I wear an ankle brace for beach volleyball?

Yes. Sand doesn't eliminate ankle sprain risk — landing in a divot or on a teammate's foot still happens. Lace-up braces work fine on sand. Some beach players prefer compression sleeves for the lighter feel, but a lace-up offers more meaningful protection.
