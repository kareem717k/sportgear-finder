What to Pack for a Ski Trip

Updated September 2026 ֲ· 8 min read ֲ· Affiliate links may earn us a commission

Home / Ski / What to Pack for a Ski Trip

Ski packing goes wrong in a specific and predictable way. People pack carefully for the mountain and forget that the trip has an airport at each end, a hotel room with no drying space in the middle, and a lot of walking in ski boots that were designed for none of it.

This is the list, organised by where each thing should actually travel.

Carry This On: The Things You Cannot Replace

The governing principle is simple. Rental shops have skis and poles in every length. What they cannot conjure on the morning your luggage did not arrive is a boot that fits your foot, or a helmet you trust.

  • Ski boots - the one item where a substitute genuinely does not work. Most boot bags are sized to pass as cabin baggage for exactly this reason.
  • Helmet and goggles - bulky but light, and both are easily crushed in a hold. They fit in the boot bag alongside the boots.
  • Gloves - small, and the single most annoying thing to replace at resort prices.
  • One base layer set and one pair of ski socks - enough to ski your first day if the hold bag is delayed.

This has a second benefit: moving boots out of the ski bag usually keeps it under the airline's weight limit, which is where the extra charges begin.

In the Hold

  • Skis and poles, in a padded bag if you own them - an unpadded sleeve protects against scratches, not against being dropped onto a conveyor.
  • Ski jacket and trousers - bulky, robust, and nothing bad happens to them in a hold.
  • Mid layers - a fleece or light down piece. Two is plenty.
  • Three pairs of ski socks - see below, this is the number people get wrong.
  • Evening clothes and proper winter boots - resort villages are icy and ski boots are miserable to walk in.

How Many Socks: Three

Two is the intuitive answer and it is one short. Merino dries slowly in a hotel room with no airing cupboard, so with two pairs you will put on a damp sock at least once during the week - and a damp sock is where cold feet and blisters both start.

Three lets one dry fully while you rotate the other two. It is why three-packs exist in this category.

The Things Everyone Forgets

  • Sunscreen, high factor - altitude plus snow reflection burns faster than a beach, and the underside of your nose and chin get it worst.
  • Lip balm with SPF - the single most common regret by day three.
  • A neck gaiter - worn below the goggle foam, not tucked under it, or it funnels your breath onto the lens and fogs it.
  • A small backpack for the mountain - water, a layer, and somewhere to put the gloves you took off.
  • Blister plasters - rental boots and new boots both cause them, and resort pharmacies know it.
  • A power bank - cold destroys phone batteries, and your lift pass or trail map may be on it.

Packing the Bags Themselves

Check the ski bag length against your skis rather than your memory - bag sizes quote internal length, so a 185cm bag genuinely will not close over 190cm skis, and bindings add bulk that can stop a tight bag zipping even when the length is right.

Get a boot bag with vented boot compartments. Liners hold a startling amount of sweat, and a week of damp boots sealed inside an airtight bag is precisely where the smell people associate with ski boots comes from. Pull the liners out to dry in the room each evening as well.

Before you buy either bag, check what your airline charges. Carriage rules vary more for skis than for any other sports equipment: some carriers take a ski bag plus a boot bag as a single checked item, some charge a flat sports-equipment fee, and some count each bag separately and charge twice.

The short version

Carry on boots, helmet, goggles, gloves and one set of base layers and socks - those are the things you cannot replace if the hold bag is delayed. Skis, poles, outerwear and the rest go in the hold. Pack three pairs of ski socks, not two. Use a boot bag with vented compartments, check your ski bag length against your actual skis, and check the airline's ski charges before you buy anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I carry on rather than check for a ski trip?

Ski boots, helmet, goggles, gloves, and one set of base layers and socks. Rental shops have skis and poles in every length, but they cannot produce a boot that fits your foot at short notice, and a helmet with an unknown history is not one you should wear. Carrying boots also usually keeps the checked ski bag under the airline weight limit.

How many pairs of ski socks do I need for a week?

Three. Merino dries slowly in a hotel room without an airing cupboard, so two pairs means putting on a damp sock at least once - and damp socks are where cold feet and blisters both begin. Three lets one dry completely while you rotate the other two, which is why three-packs are common in this category.

Do I need a padded ski bag for flying?

If you own the skis, yes. An unpadded bag keeps edges off your car seats and water off other luggage, but it offers essentially nothing against the impacts skis take in an aircraft hold. Fully padded means foam right around the bag, not a reinforcing strip along the base. For driving to the hill, unpadded is all you need.

What do people always forget to pack for skiing?

High-factor sunscreen and SPF lip balm, which matter more at altitude with snow reflecting from below than they do on a beach. After that: a neck gaiter, blister plasters, a power bank for a phone that cold will drain, and proper winter boots for walking around an icy resort village in the evening.

How do I stop my ski boots smelling on a trip?

Pull the liners out every evening and let them dry in the room rather than sealed in the bag, and use a boot bag with vented boot compartments so anything still damp can breathe in transit. The smell associated with ski boots is almost entirely the result of wet liners spending a week zipped inside an airtight bag.
