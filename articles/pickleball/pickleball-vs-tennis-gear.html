Pickleball vs Tennis Gear

Updated August 2026 ֲ· 4 min read ֲ· Affiliate links may earn us a commission

Home / Pickleball / Pickleball vs Tennis Gear

A lot of people arrive at pickleball from tennis, and the natural question is how much of the existing kit bag still works. The short answer: shoes, bags and grips transfer almost completely. Rackets and balls do not transfer at all.

What Transfers Directly

  • Shoes. Tennis shoes are the closest thing to a pickleball shoe there is — both sports demand lateral stability, hard stops and abrasion-resistant outsoles. Many dedicated pickleball shoes are lightly modified tennis shoes.
  • Bags. A tennis bag carries paddles happily. The only difference is that pickleball paddle sleeves are shorter and better shaped, so paddles slide around less.
  • Overgrips. Identical product. Tourna, Wilson Pro and HEAD Xtremesoft are used across both sports with no modification at all.
  • Court sense. Not gear, but worth saying: the split step, the volley technique and the movement patterns carry over more than the equipment does.

What Does Not Transfer

A tennis racket is strung, far larger and completely illegal in pickleball — there is no substitute, and no adaptation. Tennis balls are heavier, pressurised and bounce far higher; a pickleball is a moulded plastic ball with holes and behaves nothing like one. And a tennis net is 42 inches at the posts against pickleball's 36, and wider than the court, so a tennis net cannot simply be reused unless the facility has an adjustable system.

The Adjustment Tennis Players Find Hardest

It is not the gear, it is the paddle having no strings. A tennis racket's string bed does an enormous amount of work generating spin and absorbing pace. A solid paddle face does neither, which is why tennis players often arrive hitting long for the first few sessions — the shots that a string bed would have brought down simply do not come down. This is also why paddle face material matters so much in pickleball: it is the only source of grip on the ball.

Closest to a Tennis Feel

JOOLA Ben Johns Hyperion Raw Carbon

If you are used to generating spin with a string bed, a raw carbon face is the closest a paddle gets. The grit holds the ball long enough that brushing up the back of it produces genuine topspin, and the elongated shape gives leverage on serves that feels more familiar coming from tennis. It is unforgiving of poor contact, which is the trade — but tennis players tend to have the contact quality to justify it sooner than complete beginners.

The Short Version

Keep your tennis shoes, your bag and your overgrips. Buy a paddle and the right balls for your surface, and expect the ball to fly longer than your instincts predict for the first few sessions. Do not try to adapt a racket or reuse tennis balls — neither works, and neither is legal.

One Genuine Warning

Tennis players often start pickleball assuming it is gentler and therefore safer. The rallies are shorter and the court is smaller, but the direction changes are sharper and more frequent, and a lot of play happens at the kitchen line with very little reaction time. Court shoes matter just as much here as they do in tennis, and possibly more.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use tennis shoes for pickleball?

Yes, and they are the best substitute there is. Tennis and pickleball make near-identical demands on a shoe: lateral stability, hard stops, and outsoles that survive hard courts. What you should not use is a running, walking or gym trainer.

Can I use a tennis racket for pickleball?

No. A tennis racket is strung and far too large, and it is not legal in any form of pickleball. Paddles are solid-faced and a specific size. There is no adaptation that works.

Are tennis balls and pickleballs interchangeable?

Not at all. A tennis ball is pressurised, felt-covered and bounces much higher; a pickleball is moulded plastic with holes and a low, quick bounce. The two play nothing alike and pickleball rules require a proper pickleball.

Is a tennis net the right height for pickleball?

No. A tennis net is 42 inches at the posts; pickleball is 36 at the posts and 34 at centre, on a narrower court. Some facilities have adjustable systems, but usually the answer is a portable pickleball net set up inside the tennis court.

Why do tennis players hit long in pickleball at first?

Because a paddle has no string bed. In tennis, the strings generate spin that brings the ball down inside the baseline. A solid paddle face produces far less spin, so shots that would have dipped in tennis carry long until you adjust your swing path and pace.
