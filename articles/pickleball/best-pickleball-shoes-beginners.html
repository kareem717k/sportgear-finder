Best Pickleball Shoes for Beginners (2026)

Updated July 2026 ֲ· 5 min read ֲ· Affiliate links may earn us a commission

If you are going to spend money on one thing after a paddle, make it shoes. Running shoes are the most common piece of kit on a pickleball court and the most likely to hurt you: they are built to move you forwards, and pickleball moves you sideways on almost every point.

Why Running Shoes Are the Wrong Tool

A running shoe has a narrow, tall, soft midsole optimised for repeated forward impact. Load that sideways — which is exactly what a split step and a lateral shuffle do — and the whole shoe rolls over at the ankle. The outsole rubber is also compounded for road, not for the abrasion of a hard court, so it wears through fast. Court shoes sit lower, have a wider flatter base, a reinforced upper that resists lateral shear, and a non-marking sole.

Best for Comfort

Skechers Viper Court Reload

The best-selling pickleball shoe in the US by volume, and it earns that on comfort. The midsole is noticeably plusher than anything else near this price, which is why it is the standard recommendation for players with knee complaints or anyone playing long social sessions. Lateral support is adequate rather than exceptional — this is a comfort shoe first — but it is a genuine court shoe and a very easy transition from trainers.

Best for Stability

K-Swiss Men's Express Light Pickleball

The pick if you would rather feel locked in than cushioned. The ride is firmer than the Skechers, which some people love and others find harsh after two hours. Available in a wide fitting, which matters more in court shoes than most people expect.

The Short Version

Any court shoe beats any running shoe. If you want comfort and play mostly socially, take the Skechers Viper Court. If you want stability and play competitively, take the K-Swiss Express Light. If money is tight, the FILA Volley Zone is a real court shoe at a low price. And if you have wide feet, buy a wide fitting rather than sizing up — court shoes are cut deliberately narrow.

Indoor and Outdoor Wear Very Differently

Outdoor courts are concrete or asphalt and destroy soft outsoles in weeks. Indoor gym floors are far gentler but demand a genuinely non-marking sole that grips polished wood. If you play mostly outdoors, prioritise outsole durability above everything else — some manufacturers offer an outsole guarantee, which tells you exactly where they expect the wear to happen.

Replace Them Before They Look Finished

The support in a court shoe fails before the upper looks worn out. If the midsole has compressed flat, or you can twist the shoe easily along its length, the lateral support has gone even if there is tread left. Playing three or four times a week outdoors, six to nine months is realistic. Judging by appearance will keep you in a shoe that stopped protecting you months ago.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wear running shoes for pickleball?

You can, but you should not. Running shoes have tall, soft, narrow midsoles designed for forward motion, and they roll at the ankle under the lateral load pickleball creates on every point. Rolled ankles and rapidly shredded outsoles are both avoidable with a proper court shoe.

Are tennis shoes good for pickleball?

Yes — they are the closest thing there is. Tennis and pickleball make almost identical demands: lateral movement, hard stops, abrasive hard courts. Many dedicated pickleball shoes are lightly modified tennis shoes. If you already own tennis shoes, use them.

Do I need wide pickleball shoes?

If you normally buy wide shoes, yes. Court shoes are cut narrow on purpose so your foot does not slide inside the shoe during lateral moves. Sizing up in a standard width is the wrong fix — it adds length without adding width where you actually need it.

How often should I replace pickleball shoes?

Every six to nine months if you play three or four times a week outdoors, and longer indoors. Judge by support rather than tread: if the shoe twists easily or the midsole feels dead and flat, the protection has gone regardless of how the outsole looks.

What is the difference between indoor and outdoor pickleball shoes?

Mostly the outsole rubber. Outdoor shoes use a harder, abrasion-resistant compound with a more aggressive tread to survive concrete. Indoor shoes use a softer gum-style non-marking rubber that grips polished wood. Outdoor shoes work indoors but can feel slippery; indoor shoes wear out fast outside.
