Best Swimming Training Equipment (2026)

Updated July 2026 · 8 min read · Affiliate links may earn us a commission

Walk any club pool deck and you'll see the same four tools beside every lane: fins, a pull buoy, a kickboard, and paddles. That's not tradition — it's because each one isolates a different piece of the stroke so you can train it harder or fix it. Everything else in the swim-gadget aisle is optional.

This guide covers what each tool actually does, the order to buy them in, and the specific models worth your money — every one of them standard club-swimmer kit, not gimmicks.

Buy Second · Pull Buoy

Speedo Pull Buoy

Twelve dollars of foam that isolates your entire upper-body stroke. Also the tool that lets tired legs rest mid-session while you keep swimming. There's nothing to get wrong in a pull buoy at this price; Speedo's is the deck standard. Paddles enlarge your hand, multiplying both the workload and the feedback on every pull — brilliant for strength and catch refinement, brutal on shoulders if your technique isn't ready. What Each Tool Actually Trains

Fins — body position and kick conditioning

The propulsion boost lifts your hips to the surface, letting you feel what correct body position is like — then you chase that feeling without the fins. Short-blade training fins also turn kick sets into genuine conditioning instead of slow-motion frustration.

Pull buoy — upper-body isolation

Legs off, arms only. Use it to focus completely on catch, pull path, and rotation. Common mistake: swimming every session with it because it feels easier. It's a scalpel, not a flotation aid.

Kickboard — leg isolation

The inverse of the buoy. Kick sets hurt and get skipped, which is precisely why swimmers with strong kicks separate themselves in the last ten meters of every race.

Paddles — strength and feel

More surface area means more resistance per stroke and unmissable feedback when your hand slips or your catch collapses. Earn them with a few months of consistent technique work first.

What about snorkels, tempo trainers, and parachutes?

All legitimate — all optional. A center-mount snorkel is the best of the second wave (it removes breathing from the equation while you drill), a tempo trainer suits data-driven swimmers, and resistance gear belongs in coached programs. None comes before the core four.

Frequently Asked Questions

What swimming equipment should a beginner buy first?

After goggles and a suit: fins, then a pull buoy, then a kickboard. Fins fix more beginner problems than any other tool. Paddles come last — they punish flawed technique before you're ready for them.

Do training aids actually make you faster?

Used purposefully, yes — each isolates part of the stroke for overload or correction. Used as crutches to make every length easier, they make you fast with equipment and unchanged without it. Structure matters more than gear.

Is a training bundle worth it?

Arena's kickboard + pull buoy bundle is cheaper than buying separately and club quality. Buy pieces individually only when you have specific preferences, like an alignment board over a classic kickboard.
