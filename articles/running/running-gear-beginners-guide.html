Running Gear: Complete Beginner Guide

Updated September 2026 ֲ· 8 min read ֲ· Affiliate links may earn us a commission

Home / Running / Running Gear: Complete Beginner Guide

Running has the shortest essential kit list of any sport we cover, and the longest list of things people will try to sell you. The honest version is this: you need one pair of proper running shoes. Everything else either fixes a specific problem you may or may not have, or makes running more pleasant once you are doing it regularly.

That is good news for beginners, because it means the right way to start is to spend money on one thing, go running for a few weeks, and let the problems you actually run into tell you what to buy next.

The One Essential: Shoes That Fit

A running shoe is designed to cushion repeated landings and roll you forward smoothly. Fashion trainers, tennis shoes and old gym shoes do neither well, and running in them is the fastest way to sore shins. You do not need an expensive shoe to start — you need a neutral, moderately cushioned running shoe that fits.

Fit means a thumb's width of space in front of your longest toe, no heel slip when you walk, and nothing pressing on the widest part of your foot. If a shoe needs "breaking in", it is the wrong shoe. The beginner shoe guide has specific picks, and neutral vs stability explains the one technical decision worth understanding.

Two Cheap Things That Fix Most Complaints

Running socks, not cotton

Most beginner blisters come from cotton socks, which hold sweat against the skin and loosen when wet. A multi-pack of synthetic running socks costs about the same as a sandwich and fixes the problem outright. See how to stop blisters if you already have them.

Somewhere to put your phone

A phone in a shorts pocket bounces, swings and drags your waistband down. A simple running belt holds it at your waist where it barely moves. It sounds like a luxury; after one run with a phone in your pocket, most people buy one.

What Can Wait

  • A GPS watch. Your phone's free running app tracks distance and pace well enough for your first months. Buy a watch when you know you will keep running.
  • Water on the run. For runs under an hour in normal weather, drink beforehand and you are fine. Hydration gear matters for long runs and hot days.
  • Technical clothing. Any non-cotton T-shirt and shorts will do. Cotton gets heavy and chafes, but you probably already own synthetic sportswear.
  • Recovery tools. A massage ball for under $10 is worth having once you are running several times a week. Rollers can come later.

If You Run in the Dark

The exception to "it can wait" is visibility. If your runs are before or after work in winter, something reflective and a light are safety kit, not accessories — most running clothes are black, and drivers genuinely cannot see you. Running in the dark covers what works.

The short version

Buy one pair of properly fitting running shoes. Add synthetic running socks and a belt for your phone when you notice you need them, which will be soon. Leave watches, hydration and recovery tools until running is a habit - and if you run in the dark, get a reflective vest and a light straight away.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do I need to start running?

A pair of running shoes that fit well. That is the only essential. Synthetic running socks and a belt for your phone are cheap additions that fix the most common beginner complaints, blisters and a bouncing phone. Everything else can wait until you are running regularly.

How much should beginner running shoes cost?

A proper entry-level running shoe costs around $75 to $100. Mid-range daily trainers around $150 use better foam that stays cushioned for more miles. You do not need an expensive shoe to start, but you do need a running shoe rather than a fashion or tennis shoe.

Do I need a running watch as a beginner?

No. A free app on your phone tracks distance, pace and time well enough for the first months. A GPS watch is convenient and more accurate, but it makes more sense once you know running is going to stick.

Can I run in normal gym clothes?

Yes, as long as they are not cotton. Cotton absorbs sweat, gets heavy and chafes. Most synthetic sportswear works fine for running. Dedicated running clothing adds things like better ventilation and pockets, but it is not necessary to start.

What is the most common beginner running gear mistake?

Running in the wrong shoes - fashion trainers, old gym shoes or tennis shoes - followed closely by cotton socks. The first causes sore shins and knees; the second causes blisters. Both are cheap to fix.
