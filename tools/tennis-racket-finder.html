Tennis Racket Finder

Racket marketing talks about power and feel. What actually determines how a frame plays is four numbers. Answer five questions and get the specs that fit your game — then match them to real rackets.

Short answer: if you are new, look for a 100–110 sq in head, 260–285 g strung, light or even balance, pre-strung, under $60. Bigger head means bigger sweet spot, and forgiveness beats control until your contact point is consistent.

The four specs that actually matter

Head size — forgiveness vs control

The single biggest factor in how a racket plays. Oversized heads (105–115 sq in) have large sweet spots and add free depth, which is exactly what you want while your contact is still inconsistent. Mid-plus heads (98–100 sq in) reward clean technique with control and feel. Rule of thumb: if you mishit often, go bigger; if you are deliberately shaping shots, go smaller.

Weight — stability vs manoeuvrability

Light frames (250–275 g) are easy to swing but get pushed around by a heavy ball. Heavier frames (295–320 g) plough through contact and stay stable, but demand earlier preparation and more fitness. Most adult improvers land around 280–300 g strung. You can always add lead tape; you cannot remove frame weight.

Balance — where the weight sits

Head-heavy frames put mass out at the tip, which adds power to a short swing — useful for beginners and doubles players. Head-light frames are quicker through the air and easier to control on fast swings. Even balance splits the difference.

String pattern — spin vs durability

An open pattern (16×19) lets the strings move and bite the ball, generating more spin, at the cost of faster string wear. A dense pattern (18×20) gives more control and lasts longer but produces less spin for the same swing.

If you have tennis elbow

The intuition that a lighter racket is gentler is backwards. Very light, very stiff frames transmit more shock into the arm, because there is less mass to absorb the impact and less flex to soften it. A slightly heavier, more flexible frame with a softer string at lower tension is the combination that helps. String choice often matters more than the frame — switching from a stiff polyester to a multifilament is the cheapest thing to try first.

Frequently asked questions

What head size should a beginner racket have?

100 to 110 sq in. The larger sweet spot matters far more than control at that stage.

How heavy should my racket be?

Most adult improvers do best between 280 and 300 g strung.

Does an expensive racket make you play better?

Not by itself. Below about $100 you are buying basic playability; above that you are buying fit to your particular swing. A $60 frame that matches your game beats a $250 frame that does not.

Pre-strung or unstrung?

Budget rackets come pre-strung and ready to play. Performance frames usually come unstrung so you can choose string and tension — budget $20–40 extra for stringing.
