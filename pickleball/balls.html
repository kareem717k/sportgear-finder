Pickleballs are not interchangeable, and using the wrong one is the fastest way to make a good paddle feel bad. Outdoor balls have 40 small holes, thicker walls and more mass, so wind moves them less and they fly faster and harder. Indoor balls have around 26 larger holes, thinner walls and a softer feel, so they travel slower and spin more. Buy for the surface you actually play on — outdoor balls indoors feel harsh and skid, and indoor balls outdoors get blown around and crack quickly in the cold.

Choosing the Right Pickleball

Indoor and Outdoor Are Genuinely Different Balls

This is the single most common beginner mistake. Outdoor balls have 40 small holes and heavier, harder plastic so wind does not push them around. Indoor balls have about 26 larger holes and softer plastic so they stay controllable on a slick gym floor. Swap them and the game changes completely — an outdoor ball on a gym floor skids and stings, an indoor ball outside is unplayable in any breeze.

Cracking Is Normal, and Cold Makes It Worse

Every pickleball eventually cracks; that is the nature of moulded plastic being hit hard. Cold weather makes it dramatically worse, because the plastic gets brittle. If you play outdoors below about 10C, expect to go through balls faster and buy a ball specifically rated for cold. A cracked ball is not just softer, it flies unpredictably — bin it.

Check It Is USA Pickleball Approved

If you play in any organised league or tournament, the ball needs to be on the USA Pickleball approved list. Every ball here is. For back-garden play it does not matter, but approved balls also tend to be more consistent between batches, which is worth having even socially.

Buy More Than You Think

Balls are consumable. A three-pack lasts a serious outdoor player a few weeks at most. Buying a bulk pack is substantially cheaper per ball and means you are never tempted to keep playing with a cracked one, which is how bad habits and inconsistent contact creep in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between indoor and outdoor pickleballs?

Outdoor balls have 40 small holes, thicker walls and more weight, so wind affects them less and they play fast and hard. Indoor balls have around 26 larger holes and softer, thinner plastic, so they are slower, quieter and easier to control on a gym floor. They are not interchangeable and playing with the wrong one changes the game noticeably.

How long does a pickleball last?

Outdoors, a ball might last a few sessions before it cracks or goes soft — sometimes a single cold morning. Indoors they last considerably longer because the surface and temperature are kinder. Once a ball cracks, or you can squeeze it noticeably out of round, replace it: the flight becomes unpredictable.

Why do pickleballs crack so easily?

They are single-piece moulded plastic being struck hard, repeatedly, often on abrasive outdoor courts. Cold makes the plastic brittle and accelerates it. Some outdoor balls are specifically formulated to stay flexible in low temperatures, which is worth paying for if you play through winter.

Can I use an outdoor ball indoors?

You can, but it plays badly. The heavier, harder outdoor ball skids on a smooth gym floor, comes off the paddle faster than you expect and stings on the hands. If the venue supplies balls, use theirs — most indoor venues have standardised on an indoor ball for good reason.

What colour pickleball should I buy?

Whatever contrasts most with your court surface and background. Yellow and neon green are the safest defaults outdoors; on a dark indoor floor a lighter ball is easier to track. Colour has no effect on flight, only on how well you see it.
