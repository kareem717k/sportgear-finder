The paddle is the only piece of pickleball kit that changes how the game feels. Three things decide that: face material (fibreglass for pop, graphite for control, raw carbon for spin), core thickness (13mm plays fast and punchy, 16mm plays soft and forgiving), and shape (standard for an easy sweet spot, elongated for reach at the cost of forgiveness). Beginners should ignore price and buy a 16mm standard-shape paddle — the extra forgiveness is worth more than any technology on the market.

How to Choose a Pickleball Paddle

Core Thickness Matters More Than Brand

A 16mm core flexes more on contact, which slows the ball down and makes dinks and resets far easier to control. A 13mm core is punchier and rewards players who already have hands at the net. If you are under a 4.0 rating, 16mm is almost always the better buy — the forgiveness shows up on every mis-hit.

Face Material Sets the Ceiling

Fibreglass faces are the cheapest and give the most raw pop, which flatters beginners but makes touch shots harder. Graphite is the middle ground: quieter, more predictable, easier to place. Raw carbon fibre grips the ball long enough to generate genuine spin, which is why every premium paddle now uses it — but it only pays off once you can brush the ball deliberately.

Shape Is a Trade, Not an Upgrade

Standard shape gives the widest sweet spot and is the most forgiving on off-centre hits. Elongated paddles add roughly half an inch of reach and more leverage on serves and overheads, but narrow the sweet spot and add swing weight. Singles players and taller people tend to prefer elongated; doubles players who live at the kitchen line usually do not.

Grip Size and Weight

Most paddles ship with a 4.125 to 4.25 inch grip, which suits most adults. If it feels too small you can build it up with an overgrip; you cannot make a grip smaller. Total weight between 7.8 and 8.3 ounces suits most players — lighter is faster at the net, heavier is more stable against pace.

Frequently Asked Questions

What paddle should a complete beginner buy?

A 16mm standard-shape paddle with a graphite or fibreglass face. The thicker core gives you a bigger, softer sweet spot, which matters far more at the start than spin potential. Avoid the wooden paddles that come in cheap net sets — they are heavy, dead and teach bad habits.

Is an expensive pickleball paddle actually worth it?

Only once your technique is repeatable. A premium raw carbon paddle generates more spin and a more predictable response, but it also punishes sloppy contact. Below a 3.5 rating you will get more improvement from lessons and court time than from a paddle upgrade.

What does USAPA approved mean?

It means the paddle appears on USA Pickleball's approved equipment list and is legal for sanctioned tournament play. If you only play socially it does not matter, but every paddle worth buying carries the approval anyway, so there is no reason to buy one that does not.

13mm or 16mm core - which is better?

Neither is better outright. 16mm is softer, more controlled and more forgiving, which suits most recreational and improving players. 13mm is faster off the face and better for players who want to counter-attack hard at the net and already have the hands to do it.

How long does a pickleball paddle last?

Playing three or four times a week, expect 12 to 24 months before the core softens and the face loses grit. You will notice it as balls dying short and spin dropping off. Raw carbon faces lose their texture faster than painted ones, which is the trade for the extra bite when new.
