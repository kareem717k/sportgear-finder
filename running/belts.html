Nearly everyone runs with a phone now, and a phone in a shorts pocket is miserable: it swings, it bounces and it drags your waistband down with every stride. The fix is a carrier that holds it close to the body's centre of mass, which is why a snug waist belt is the answer for most people and an armband for the rest. The thing to judge is bounce. A belt that moves a few millimetres with each step becomes intolerable after 20 minutes, so fit and design matter far more than capacity. Below are six, from a $13 armband to the tube belts that most regular runners end up in.

How to Choose a Running Belt

Belt or Armband?

A waist belt carries the load at your centre of mass, where it bounces least, and has room for keys, cards and gels as well as a phone. An armband keeps the screen visible and touchable, which suits people who follow a running app or change music a lot, but it puts weight on one arm and today's large phones make armbands feel heavy. For most runners, a belt is the better answer. Choose an armband if you want the screen in sight.

Bounce Is the Only Real Test

Every belt holds a phone when you are standing still. What separates them is whether it stays put at running pace. A belt should sit snug on the hips or low waist — tight enough not to move, loose enough to breathe — and the load should sit at the front or the small of the back. Wider, stretchier bands like the FlipBelt spread the pressure and bounce least. Narrow belts with a single pouch depend on getting the tension exactly right.

Tube Belts Need the Right Size

Buckle-free tube belts like the FlipBelt are sized, not adjustable, and a tube belt that is too big will bounce however you wear it. Measure where you will actually wear it — hips or waist — and follow the brand's size chart for that position. If you are between sizes, choose the smaller one, because the fabric relaxes a little with use.

Think About What Else You Carry

A phone is the obvious thing. The less obvious ones are a car key or house key you genuinely cannot lose, a card for emergencies, and gels on long runs. A zipped pocket is the only way to be sure about a key. If you are running long enough to need water too, a hydration belt or vest will carry everything at once.

Race Day Features

Some belts include bib toggles, so you can clip your race number to the belt instead of putting pins through your shirt. That makes it easy to move the number when you strip off a layer, and it is a small thing that is surprisingly welcome at the start line. The SPIbelt range and many hydration belts offer it; for most training runs it does not matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best way to carry a phone while running?

For most runners, a snug waist belt worn low on the hips or at the small of the back. It carries the phone close to your centre of mass, which is where it moves least, and it leaves room for keys and a card. An armband is the alternative if you want to see and touch the screen. Shorts pockets and hand-carrying are the least comfortable options on anything but very short runs.

Do running belts bounce?

A well-fitting one should not. Bounce usually comes from a belt that is too loose, too narrow or overloaded. Wide, stretchy tube belts spread the load and move least; narrow pouch belts work well when tightened properly. If a belt bounces, try wearing it lower on the hips and snugger, and move the heaviest item to the back.

Will my phone fit in a running belt?

Almost all modern phones fit in the belts on this page, including large models - check the listing's stated maximum size if your phone is unusually big or in a thick case. Armbands are more restrictive, and the phone's case can make the difference. The expandable SPIbelt pocket and the FlipBelt tubes are the most forgiving.

Is a running armband or belt better?

A belt is more comfortable for most people, carries more and bounces less. An armband wins only if you need the screen in view - for example to follow navigation or an interval app. With today's larger, heavier phones, many people who start with an armband move to a belt.

How should a FlipBelt fit?

Snugly, with no sagging when loaded. It is not adjustable, so the size is everything: measure around the point where you will wear it - lower on the hips needs a larger size than at the natural waist - and follow FlipBelt's size chart. If you are between sizes, go smaller.
