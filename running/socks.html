Socks are the cheapest fix for the most common running complaint, which is blisters. Blisters come from friction plus moisture: a sock that holds sweat softens the skin, and a sock that slides or bunches rubs it. Cotton does both, which is why a pack of cotton sports socks is the single worst thing most new runners wear. A synthetic or merino running sock wicks sweat away, fits snugly enough not to move, and has no bulky seam across the toes. That is genuinely all you need to know. Below are six, from a six-pack that costs less than one pair of the premium socks, to a merino pair with a lifetime guarantee.

How to Choose Running Socks

Cotton Is the Problem, Not the Solution

Cotton absorbs sweat and holds it against your skin, and wet skin blisters far more easily than dry skin. It also loses its shape when damp, so the sock loosens and starts to slide inside the shoe — the second ingredient of a blister. Any sock sold for running will be a polyester, nylon or merino blend that moves moisture away from the skin instead. If you change nothing else about your kit, change this.

Cushion Is a Preference, Fit Is Not

Thin socks give more feel for the ground and leave more room in the shoe; thicker, cushioned socks add comfort under the ball of the foot and the heel. Neither is right. What matters is that the sock fits closely with no wrinkles, because a wrinkle is a friction point. One caution: if you switch from thin to thick socks, your shoes can suddenly feel a half-size smaller. Try new socks with the shoes you run in.

No-Show Socks Need a Heel Tab

Very low socks look tidy and are cooler in summer, but a no-show sock without a raised tab at the back lets the shoe's heel collar rub straight onto your Achilles. Every no-show pick on this page has a tab for that reason. If you get blisters on the back of the heel specifically, a tab or a quarter-length sock is usually the fix.

When Merino Earns Its Price

Merino wool manages moisture and temperature better than synthetics: it stays warm when damp, feels less clammy, and resists odour for longer. That makes it genuinely worth it for winter running, long runs and multi-day trips. For short summer runs a good synthetic sock is just as effective and cheaper. The Darn Tough also carries a lifetime guarantee, which changes the maths if you tend to wear through socks.

Blisters That Persist Are Usually the Shoe

If you have tried good socks and still blister in the same spot, the cause is almost always the shoe — wrong size, wrong width, or a seam in the wrong place. See the running shoe guide for fit checks. Lacing tweaks such as a heel-lock loop fix a lot of heel slippage for free.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do running socks really make a difference?

For blisters, yes - more than almost anything else you can buy. Running socks are made from materials that move sweat off the skin and are knitted to fit closely without bunching, which removes both causes of blisters: moisture and friction. Cotton sports socks do the opposite. The difference is noticeable from the first run.

Are thick or thin socks better for running?

Neither is objectively better. Thin socks give more ground feel and leave more room in the shoe; cushioned socks add comfort under the heel and forefoot. Pick by preference and by how your shoes fit - moving to a thicker sock can make a well-fitting shoe feel tight. What matters in either case is a close, wrinkle-free fit.

Is merino wool good for running socks?

Very good, especially in cold weather and on long runs. Merino stays warm when wet, feels drier against the skin, and resists odour far better than synthetics. It is more expensive and, in thin summer socks, the advantage over a good synthetic is smaller. Most merino running socks are blended with nylon for durability.

How do I stop getting blisters when running?

Start with non-cotton running socks that fit snugly, and make sure your shoes are the right length and width. If blisters persist in one spot, look at the shoe first - a heel-lock lacing loop fixes most heel slippage. Anti-chafe balm on known hotspots helps on long runs. Break up very long runs in new shoes rather than doing a half-marathon in them straight out of the box.

How many pairs of running socks do I need?

Enough to cover every run between laundry days, which for most people running three or four times a week is four to six pairs. That is why a budget multi-pack is a sensible start, with one or two premium pairs added later for long runs and cold weather.
